THE FIRST CONNECTION

Infancy: feelings begin in the body.

Infants express basic emotions before they can use words. A responsive caregiver helps the baby move from distress toward calm — the foundation of regulation and secure attachment.

0–3 moInterest, distress, and social smiling emerge.
6–12 moJoy, anger, fear; stranger and separation anxiety may appear.
18–24 moSelf-conscious feelings begin; toddlers seek autonomy.

AT BIRTH

Comfort is communication.

Newborns signal distress through crying and body movement. A calm, responsive adult helps meet physical needs and begins the rhythm of co-regulation.

WHAT YOU MAY NOTICE

Bright eyes, smiling, energetic movement, shared attention, and playful vocalisations.

WHAT HELPS

Join the positive moment, mirror the feeling, and offer warm, specific acknowledgement: “You are proud of that tower!”

NURSING NOTE

Positive affect can be an entry point for rapport, play-based assessment, and health teaching.

ATTACHMENT THEORY · BOWLBY & AINSWORTH

A secure base makes exploration possible.

When a caregiver responds sensitively and predictably, a child learns that distress can be met with comfort. This does not remove every difficult emotion; it builds confidence that feelings can be survived and shared.

IN PRACTICE

Invite a familiar caregiver into care, use consistent routines, and respond calmly to distress.
